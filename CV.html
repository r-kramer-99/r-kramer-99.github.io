Ruth L. Kramer

Ruth Kramer (she/her in workplace) is a doctoral student at the University of Virginia, where she began her course of study in the Fall of 2024. Previously, she obtained her B.A. in History and French at Williams College and her M.L.I.S. from Simmons University in May of 2024. Kramer studies 20th Century America, and her research interests include periodical and magazine cultures, lesbian feminism, and archive formation within marginalized communities. Her dissertation seeks to understand the nature of lesbian care work during the AIDS crisis in the American South and Midwest.

In addition to her research, Kramer is a Praxis Fellow at the University of Virginia Scholars' Lab, and also works at the Albemarle Charlottesville Historical Society, where she currently serves as the graduate supervisor and lead researcher on the project Contours of Community: Digitally Mapping Brown's Cove. This digital humanities project incorporates archival research, digital mapping techniques, and oral histories to tell in-depth, community-driven stories about this small section of rural Albemarle county.

Prior to beginning her studies at UVA, Kramer worked as a Special Collections post-baccalaureate fellow at Williams College, where she worked primarily as a processing and reference archivist. She currently works as a public services assitant in the Albert and Shirley Small Special Collections library where she assists researchers with their inquiries and works on library projects, such as the University Archives libguide.
